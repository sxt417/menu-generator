# Presentation modes

Choose the simplest mode that expresses the dish. Do not combine all modes in one page.

## Mode scope

Modes define editorial presentation concepts only. A mode may influence information emphasis, supporting-content selection, editorial mood, and relative composition direction. A mode must never override any global or subject-specific hard constraint. Food and beverage contracts, geometry rules, vessel preservation, typography, supporting-object limits, information restrictions, identity clusters, structural lines, and negative-space rules remain mandatory.

## A · Editorial Dish Menu

Use for a signature main, dessert, standalone beverage, or visually distinctive plate. This is the default mode. Make the dish the singular photographic anchor. Add at most 1–3 restrained ingredient or culinary cues, such as black pepper, butter, herbs, and sea salt for steak, or rice, shiso, and wasabi for sushi. Include only elements supported by the photo or user information.

Suggested emphasis: the required subject-specific identity cluster, a lightweight information block in the upper or side-upper field, the food anchor in the lower half, and at most one supported micro ingredient caption. The page should read as still-life photography with menu annotations, not as an information card above a product image.

## B · Ingredient Story Menu

Use when the relationship among the main dish, raw ingredients, and seasoning is central—especially fruit, salad, dessert, beverages, seasonal produce dishes, or ingredient-led tasting courses. Arrange a restrained still life around the primary vessel, leaving clear spatial separation. The result should feel like the story behind one dish, not a recipe diagram or lifestyle table setting.

Suggested emphasis: the required identity cluster, name and short description, central dish, 2–3 supported ingredients, then a quiet information card. The total number of supporting objects must never exceed three.

## C · Pairing Menu

Use for standalone beverages or genuinely pairing-led food and desserts where accompaniment changes the experience. Pair only with credible categories or items supported by the applicable subject contract.

Suggested emphasis: the required identity cluster, item name, hero vessel, relevant information, and pairing recommendation. Standalone beverages retain the required beverage flavor profile. Food and desserts use concise textual flavor words or tasting notes only and never use flavor bars, ticks, numeric scales, radar graphics, or structured flavor charts.

## Selection rule

- Default to A when unsure or when the dish already has a strong plate presentation.
- Choose B only when at least two meaningful supporting ingredients can be stated with confidence.
- Choose C only when pairing is genuinely useful to the item, not merely decorative.
